Instructions

Proxy Nexus provides two methods of making card proxies: paper proxies and printed cards at makeplayingcards.com.

Paper Proxies

These are cards printed on standard printer paper, either at home or at a print shop, and then sleeved with another card behind it. This is a cheap and accessible way of making proxies but requires a bit of work.

  1. Generate and download the PDF from Proxy Nexus.
  2. Print the PDF on standard paper at 100% scale (actual size).
  3. Use a guillotine cutter or scissors to cut the prints.
  4. Sleeve the cut cards along with another card behind it for support.

Printed cards from makeplayingcards.com

These are cards produced by makeplayingcards.com (MPC). Because there is some tolerance on exactly where the cards are cut, Proxy Nexus either generates or includes (depending on the source image) a bleed border around the card to make the cut edge less noticeable. Ordering from MPC takes less effort to set up and produces great cards, but is more expensive than paper proxies.

If the generated zip file contains cards with different artwork on the backs, they will be organized in separate folders. In that case, you can either set up a separate deck for each folder, or combine them all into one deck. Some cards have specific images for the back side of the card. These are in the same folder as the fronts, with names ending in "-back". If not every card in the deck has the same back image, be sure to select "Different images" on step 12 below.

How to make an MPC order

  1. Download the zip file from Proxy Nexus.
  2. Extract the zip file.
  3. Visit the Custom Game Cards (63 x 88mm) page to get started with a new project.
  4. Choose the card stock. Either "(S30) Standard Smooth" or "(S33) Superior Smooth" is a good choice.
  5. Choose the deck size.
  6. Click the red "start your design" button.
  7. Enter the exact card count for the deck (the total across all folders, if combining them) and click the blue "Different images" button for the card fronts. Don't include images ending in "-back" in the count.
  8. Click the red "Upload images" button near the top right and select all the images in the folder from the extracted zip file, except those ending in "-back". Those are placed in step 13. If combining folders into one deck, repeat this step with the other folders.
  9. Once all the images have finished uploading, click the "Help me autofill images!" button. Or simply drag and drop all the images to the cards on the left. If the deck contains cards from more than one folder, take note of the folder each card number came from. You'll need this in step 13.
  10. Once all the images have been placed, click the "Next Step" button at the top right of the screen.
  11. Click "Next Step" again to skip adding text.
  12. If every card in the deck has the same back image, select "Same Image". Otherwise, select "Different images" for the card backs.
  13. If you picked "Different images", then upload the card back images from the root of the zip file, plus the "-back" images skipped in step 8, and manually place them on their respective cards like you did in step 9. If you picked "Same Image", then upload the deck's card back image from the root of the zip file and place it in the single card preview on the left. When there's more than one folder, each card back's file name starts with the name of the folder it goes with, minus "-images" (for example, "player_original.png" goes with the "player-images" folder). If there are multiple styles of the same card back, such as "original" and "proxy", pick one.
  14. Click the "Next Step" button at the top right of the screen.
  15. Click "Next Step" again to skip adding text.
  16. You will now see a preview of the deck, showing the fronts and backs of each card. This is a good chance to review the placement of back and front images if that was done manually.
  17. Click the confirmation checkbox at the bottom of the page, and then click "Add to Cart".
  18. From here you can check out, save the design, or add another deck to your order.
